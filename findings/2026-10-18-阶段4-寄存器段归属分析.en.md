# Stage 4 · Segment attribution of the microcode's register addresses (with one important correction)

## 1. Correction: PGRAPH is not at 0x20000

Earlier, based on a secondary source, I guessed "`0x20000-0x3FFFF` = PGRAPH". **That was wrong.**

envytools `rnndb/nv_mmio.xml`, the authoritative definition:

```
<!-- 400000 - 5fffff PGRAPH -->
```

**PGRAPH is at `0x400000 - 0x5FFFFF`.** Our 379 writes (`0x20000-0x2FFFF`) have nothing to do with PGRAPH.

## 2. The 0x020000 segment as recorded by envytools

```
<!-- 020000 - 020fff PTHERM    thermal monitoring
<!-- 021000 - 021fff PFUSE     fuses
<!-- 022000 - 0223ff PUNK022   unnamed
<!-- 022400 - 0227ff PUNITS    unit enable/disable
```

**Key contradiction**: among the addresses the microcode accesses, `0x22a00 / 0x23200 / 0x23300 / 0x23800`
**all exceed `0x227FF`**.

**Conclusion: Turing's MMIO layout differs from what envytools records (which stops at Maxwell/Kepler) and
cannot be applied directly.**

## 4. Three possible explanations (pending verification)

### Hypothesis A · Turing reorganized the MMIO layout
Turing (2018) is two generations beyond envytools' rnndb coverage (which stops at Maxwell).
The `0x20000` segment may have been reassigned to GR internal submodules (GPC/TPC/SM-level configuration).
**Verify**: check whether Turing's `dev_gr.h` or `dev_gpc*.h` defines blocks at `0x2000x` offsets.

### Hypothesis B · these are "window addresses" from the Falcon's perspective
The Falcon's `iowr I[addr]` may not equal a BAR0 absolute address directly, but go through some offset/window
translation.
**Verify**: pick a known absolute address (such as FECS's own 0x409000) and see whether the microcode accesses it too.

### Hypothesis C · these are register-block indices inside the GR engine
FECS accesses GR submodules (GPC/TPC) through the `0x20000` segment as **logical indices**, not physical offsets.
**Verify**: look for write patterns that loop over the SM count (14).
